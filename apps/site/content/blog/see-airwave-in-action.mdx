Airwave is easier to *show* than to explain. So we made a short video: a quick tour of the guide, the
channels, and the player. Give it a watch above.

## What you're seeing

The whole idea in about a minute and a half: you point Airwave at your own Plex server, build channels from
your library, and get a broadcast-style guide with something always on. Tune in and you land in the middle of
whatever's "currently airing," flip through channels, rewind out of a program and back through the break into
the last one, and jump to live whenever you want. It's your media, running on your hardware, as live TV.

Everything in the video is the real app: the Aurora guide, the glass player with the DVR scrubber, the
between-program bumpers, and the same 4K and HDR direct-play the native apps do.

## Where to get it

- **Apple TV**: [live on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6801608850).
- **Fire TV**: [live on the Amazon Appstore](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HGBHTJWF).
- **Roku**: [live on the Roku Channel Store](https://channelstore.roku.com/details/713ffa078c7a5f05bd441a8560cc6d7e:fa378e9461337dd32c76013f8faa4154/airwave-tv).
- **Windows and macOS**: native desktop apps to download from [getairwave.tv](https://getairwave.tv).
- **iPad and Android TV** are working through their store reviews, and the **browser player** works everywhere today.

## And it's yours

The server, the admin, and the browser player are free and source-available to run yourself. The native store
apps are a small paid convenience for people who'd rather install from a store than sideload. Your library,
your server, your channels.

If the video made you want to try it, the [docs](https://getairwave.tv/docs) will get you from zero to your
first channel in a few minutes.
